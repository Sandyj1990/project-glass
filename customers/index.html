01 · THE 100X THESIS

The customer org becomes 100× when the agent answers first, and the human only handles the genuinely hard 2 %.

AJIO ZIP is already there — 88 % positive engagement, 86 % chat-driven discovery on 2 M+ products. Netmeds Assistant: 96 % favourable on 850 K messages. The play for the Customer org is to replicate that pattern across every brand and every touchpoint — pre-purchase, in-purchase, post-purchase, in-store.

The agent isn't a chatbot. It is the new front door — it talks, it thinks, it acts. It cancels an order, refunds a payment, swaps a size, books a try-on slot, files a warranty claim, finds an alternative when something is out of stock. The human team is freed to do the irreplaceable parts of the job — empathy, exceptions, escalation.

This is not "deflect more tickets". This is the customer's first interaction becoming a satisfying purchase decision, a calmer return, a discovered new product — without ever queueing for a human.

07 · WHERE TEAMS GET STUCK

The four objections you'll hear in the first week.

Every team that has adopted these platforms has run into the same four worries. Here is how it actually plays out.

Pitfall #1

"The agent will break for our edge cases."

Every CX team has stories of the 3 % weird scenarios their agents fail. The worry is real but the wrong frame — the question isn't whether the agent handles 100 % of the long tail, it's whether it handles 90 % of the head better than the human did.

The move: Pilot with a hard fallback to human escalation. Measure the escalation path. Tune the boundary monthly. AJIO ZIP's 88 % deflection is the new baseline — the 12 % escalations are routed to specialists who do more interesting work.
Pitfall #2

"Customers will hate talking to a bot."

This was true in 2020. The 96 % favourable on Netmeds Assistant (850 K messages) is from real Indian customers in real Indian languages on real Indian payment edge cases.

The move: The bar is not "is it perfect" — it is "is it better than waiting 6 hours for tier-1". Be honest about what the agent is. Hand off cleanly when it isn't the right tool. Measure CSAT per channel, not per technology.
Pitfall #3

"We don't have clean data for the agent."

Customer teams expect to spend 6 months "cleaning data" before agents work. They're wrong about the order. Kaily on AJIO ZIP went live before the UCP rollout was complete — and got smarter as UCP filled in.

The move: Ship Kaily on whatever identity is available today (anonymous session, email, phone). Layer UCP in week 4. The agent's value compounds with identity, but it doesn't need to wait.
Pitfall #4

"Our org is set up around tickets. The agent breaks our metrics."

Tickets-per-day is the wrong scoreboard for an agent-first CX team. Ops teams compensated on ticket volume will resist the rollout — correctly, by their incentive system.

The move: Move the scoreboard to: resolution-rate, customer-effort, repeat-rate, NPS. Re-design team structure around exception handling, agent training, and policy authorship — not queue burn-down. The CX leaders we work with do this in week 2, not week 12.
08 · FAQ

The questions you'll get from your team.

Do we keep our existing CRM / Zendesk / Freshdesk?

Yes. Kaily plugs in on top of existing CX infrastructure as the front door. Escalations route to your existing helpdesk. Over time you may shrink the CRM footprint — but never in week 1.

What languages does Kaily speak?

Production-tested in English, Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Marathi, Kannada, Bengali, Gujarati, Punjabi. Additional regional languages are a config change — talk to the Kaily team for the current catalog.

How do we measure quality, not just volume?

Default scorecard: deflection rate, CSAT, first-contact resolution, escalation reason taxonomy. The Insights surface in Kaily ships with all four. Connect to your existing NPS source for the loop.

Who owns the agent's voice / brand?

You do. Kaily uses your brand guidelines as a system prompt + style guide. The Growth team usually authors the persona; CX owns the conversational policies; product owns the action surface.

What about compliance and data residency?

Indian customer data stays in India. Kaily is built on the same SOC 2 + GDPR foundation as Boltic. PCI scope is contained — payment surfaces hand off to the existing PG.

Can we run this in a single store first?

Yes for Kio. Yes for SwapEasy. Kaily is digital-first — start with one storefront's chat window, then take it physical via Kio.
